Vision — The Getaway

A Cabin in Deep Space

Dreaming past "a room with a nice window" toward the feeling of truly being somewhere — and truly unwinding there.

The north star: a person puts on the headset after a long day and, within seconds, their shoulders drop. They are somewhere else — a warm, quiet berth adrift among the stars — and they don't want to leave. Every decision serves two feelings at once: awe (the view outside) and calm (the room around you).

00The feeling we're chasing

Relaxation in VR isn't a scene — it's a state. It comes from the body believing it's somewhere safe and beautiful: warm light close around you, something vast and slow beyond the glass, sound you can feel, and nothing that ever demands anything of you. Below is the getaway at its best — cosy foreground, breathtaking beyond.

The whole design lives in this contrast: a small pool of warm lamplight, a sleeping cat, a hot drink, a plant — and beyond the glass, a ringed world, an aurora, a moon, the quiet. Cosy foreground makes the awe safe; the awe makes the cosiness meaningful.

01Six pillars of immersion

I Presence

Your body believes it's there — your hands rest in your lap, the room breathes, arrival and departure are gentle rituals, not menu clicks.

II The Living Cabin

The room is inhabited: steam curls, a plant sways, a companion stirs, coves warm at "night." Tiny life, endlessly.

III The View

Not one planet but a living cosmos — auroras, ring-shadows sweeping the room, a moonrise over the limb, rare wonders.

IV The Soundscape

Generative, spatial, felt-in-the-chest. Evolving music per world, hull resonance, distant cosy comms, a kettle.

V Rituals of Rest

Things to do that slow you down: a breathing pacer in the cove light, a sleep timer, tea, a chess puzzle, stargazing.

VI Yours to Return To

It remembers you, rewards coming back with rare nights, and keeps a quiet logbook of wonders you've witnessed.

02The view, dreamed bigger

Today's tour of worlds is the foundation. The leap to breathtaking is making the cosmos feel deep, alive, and occasionally miraculous — and letting it spill into the room.

  • Light that fills the room. The planet and aurora tint the cabin — warm amber from Ember, cold blue from the ice giant — and a slow ring-shadow sweeps across the floor and your lap as you orbit. This single effect does more for "I'm really there" than anything else.
  • Volumetric depth. Parallax nebula layers, god-rays through cloud, dust drifting past the glass — so space reads as an enormous 3D volume, not a backdrop.
  • Signature spectacles (rare, slow, comfort-safe): a moonrise over the gas giant's limb; an aurora breathing across a night side; a comet that lingers for minutes; a distant, silent supernova bloom; a meteor shower night; a total eclipse as a moon crosses the sun.
  • Scale cues. A tiny ship or a chain of moons crossing the giant gives it real size; reflections of the planet ripple on the glass.
  • Gentle drift. The whole ship slowly rotates so the view migrates over a long session (capped well below any discomfort) — the sky is never quite the same twice.

03The living cabin

Awe is out the window; comfort is in the room. A relaxation space has to feel inhabited and gently alive, or it reads as a showroom.

Micro-motion sells presence for almost no cost: steam, a swaying plant, dust in a light shaft, a slowly turning desk globe. A companion — a cat curled on the couch that breathes and occasionally stirs, or a small pottering maintenance drone — is the single warmest touch a solo relaxation space can have. And the room should respond: dim and warm as a session winds toward sleep.

04The soundscape — the biggest lever of all

In a relaxation app, sound does more than visuals. This is where the deepest gains are.

  • Generative ambient music, unique per world — slow evolving pads that never loop audibly, tuned to each world's key (Ember warm and low, Deep Quiet almost silent). Spatialised so it surrounds you.
  • Sub-bass hull resonance you feel in your chest — the deep, grounding hum of a big quiet ship.
  • Diegetic cosiness: air handling at the vents, the occasional distant, unintelligible comms chatter (weirdly comforting — you're not alone, but undisturbed), a kettle, tea pouring, hull ticks as it warms and cools.
  • Adaptive mix: everything eases down as a session ages, so a 40-minute stay drifts naturally toward sleep.
  • Optional sleep-safe layers — brown noise, rain-on-hull, a soft heartbeat-of-the-ship.

05Rituals of rest

The difference between a pretty scene and a place you unwind is having gentle things to do — or to deliberately stop doing.

A breathing pacer — the cove light swelling and fading at ~5.5 breaths/min — is a tiny feature with an outsized calming effect. Pair it with a sleep mode (the room dims, the view slows, a timer fades you to black), a tea ritual, the existing chessboard as a quiet puzzle, and a pure stargazing mode with nothing to do at all.

06Presence, arrival & the Quest 3 flagship

  • Your hands, at rest. Hand-tracking so you glance down and see your own hands in your lap — one of the fastest ways the body accepts "I am here."
  • Arrival & departure rituals. You don't spawn; you settle in — a soft fade-up, the hum rising, the chair accepting you. Leaving is a gentle fade-down, not a hard cut.
  • The passthrough portal (the "show a friend" moment). A mixed-reality mode where your real room is visible and the window becomes a portal to space — the cabin materialising around your actual furniture. Uniquely Quest 3, uniquely wow.
  • Time that passes. A subtle in-cabin day/night as you switch worlds; the ship feels like it's really travelling.

The through-line. Everything above serves one sentence: warm within, vast without. Make the room a place the body trusts, make the cosmos a thing the eye can't quite believe, bind them with light and sound — and let the person simply be there.
Guardrails, always. Comfort first — everything vast is slow, the room is the stable frame, nothing startles. Original sci-fi only. And relaxation-safe by design: warm light, gentle sub-bass, no sudden events, an experience that always drifts toward calm.
