## M7 — Finish Great Weather as the first hero destination

The [M7 implementation](great-weather-eclipse-review.md) provides shallow cloud relief, revised ring/moon lighting and a shared eclipse. It passed merge-time Unity review; subsequent headset feedback found the event subtle. The [M8 candidate](harbour-depth-review.md) enlarges the moon and shared shadow; headset readability and device acceptance remain open.

Produce a focused comparison slice before applying changes to all five vistas.

1. **Cloud depth and natural weather structure.** Give belts broad, readable shapes, irregular storm boundaries and restrained upper/lower cloud separation. Review oblique views and the terminator for a sense of layered atmosphere. Start with precomputed detail and a tightly bounded extra sampling cost; prototype before committing to additional transparent shells.
2. **One sun, one consistent world.** Refine ring illumination, planetary night/day balance, moon lighting, atmospheric colour and shared shadows together. Rings should read as a vast thin sheet of ice and dust. Preserve black space and avoid a uniform luminous outline. Check shadow edges and subpixel detail during head movement.
3. **One memorable slow event.** Author a small moon gradually eclipsing the sun over a limited region of the planet/rings. Its position must explain the changing shadow. Build on the existing emergence event and deterministic clock, with a quiet lead-in, several-minute evolution and a settled ending. No sudden camera movement, flashing storm lights or forced attention cue. Quiet remains a stable observation mode; Living enables the event.
4. **Approve from the cabin.** Compare M6.4 and M7 from couch, both bed positions and desk. Evaluate both the first glance and a 20-minute stay. Inspect Still/Drift, both eyes, event reset and preview, plus a warm 30-minute performance run at the requested 72 Hz. Retain a measured margin within the approximately 13.9 ms frame interval rather than spending the entire budget on a single screenshot.

Deliverables: authored composition/lighting parameters, source-generated or licensed/provenanced assets, fixed-seat/event captures with build identity, a short headset comparison video and measured device timings. M7 succeeds when cloud depth, ring scale and the eclipse read naturally from multiple seats—not merely when shaders compile.

## M8 — Make the harbour feel inhabited

The [first M8 candidate](harbour-depth-review.md) implements asymmetric districts, a recessed berth, continuous tender service and slower distant traffic. Unity and headset review are pending; subsequent model/material refinement should follow that evidence.

Use the same review loop to replace the most conspicuous procedural repetition with original station architecture: large structural spans, medium docking districts and small windows/service equipment. Add asymmetry and purposeful traffic with visible destinations. A tender passing behind a major structure should communicate more scale than extra background objects. Preserve comfortable relative motion and validate all paths against the actual geometry.

Extend the established ship design into Formation after the harbour scale cues work. Improve hull silhouettes, materials, window placement and mutual occlusion before adding more ships.

Refine cloud altitude, coastline scale, the ocean highlight and the colour transition through sunrise. Add a restrained nightside aurora only where lighting and geography support it, using a bounded local effect. The event should reveal detail slowly rather than wash the view in bloom. Carry M7's coherent sunlight and per-seat review criteria into this vista.

## M10 — Make two hours feel alive without becoming a show

The existing Living experience reaches one grace event and settles; it is not yet a varied hours-long observation system. Add slow, bounded changes with long quiet intervals and deliberate nonrepetition. Retain deterministic seeds/seeks for QA, explicit Quiet reset, pause/reentry behavior, and event-preview controls. Avoid continuous orbital camera movement and frequent spectacle. Evolve restrained sound and cabin spill only when they belong to the event.

The First Question remains the dark, sparse visual baseline. Do not turn every destination into a busy scene. End this stage with sustained thermal/comfort tests, transition checks and a documented performance budget per vista.
